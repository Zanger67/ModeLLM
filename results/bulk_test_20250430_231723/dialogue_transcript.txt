=== PROPOSALS ===

[2025-04-30 23:15:53] PROPOSAL by USA:
Title: Title: Global Climate Action Plan: Implementing a Fair and Effective Global Carbon Tax
Action Item 1:
- Establish a global carbon tax framework that takes into account the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities, acknowledging the differing capacities and historical contributions to greenhouse gas emissions of developed and developing nations.
- The tax rates should be structured in a progressive manner, with developed nations bearing a higher burden initially, while providing support and incentives for technology transfer and capacity-building in developing countries.

Action Item 2:
- Create a Climate Finance Mechanism to support developing nations in their transition to low-carbon economies. This mechanism should prioritize financial assistance, technology transfer, and capacity-building to facilitate sustainable development while ensuring no disproportionate burden on vulnerable economies.
- Encourage collaboration and information-sharing on best practices in carbon pricing and emission reduction strategies to foster innovation and efficiency across nations.

Considering Different National Perspectives:
- Recognize the diverse economic and developmental needs of nations by offering flexibility in carbon tax implementation, allowing for tailored approaches that align with national circumstances and priorities.
- Ensure transparency and accountability in the distribution of funds generated from the carbon tax to guarantee fairness and effectiveness in addressing climate challenges.

Political Viability:
- Seek consensus through ongoing dialogues and negotiations, fostering a spirit of cooperation and trust among nations.
- Emphasize the economic benefits of a coordinated global carbon tax system, highlighting the potential for innovation, job creation, and sustainable growth.

This proposal calls for a balanced and inclusive approach to climate action, addressing the concerns of both developed and developing nations while laying the foundation for a more sustainable and resilient future for all.

[2025-04-30 23:15:57] PROPOSAL by China:
Title: Title: Framework for Equitable Implementation of a Global Carbon Tax
Action Items:
1. Differentiated Tax Rates: Implement a global carbon tax with differentiated rates based on countries' historical emissions, current capacities, and development levels. Developed countries would have higher tax rates to account for their historical contributions to emissions, while developing nations would have lower rates to support their economic growth and sustainable development goals.
   
2. Climate Finance Mechanism: Establish a dedicated fund within the global carbon tax framework to provide financial assistance to developing countries for climate adaptation, mitigation efforts, and technology transfer. The fund would be replenished by contributions from developed nations in proportion to their carbon tax revenues, ensuring equitable support for vulnerable countries.
   
3. Technology Transfer and Capacity Building: Facilitate technology transfer and capacity-building initiatives, particularly for developing countries, to accelerate the adoption of clean technologies and enhance their resilience to climate impacts. Encourage partnerships between developed and developing nations to promote knowledge sharing and innovation in sustainable practices.
    
Considering Different National Perspectives:
- Developed Nations: Align the global carbon tax implementation with their commitments under international climate agreements, emphasizing the need for ambitious emission reductions and financial support for developing countries.
- Developing Nations: Ensure that the carbon tax framework accommodates their developmental needs and provides adequate financial and technological assistance to pursue sustainable development pathways.
    
Political Viability:
This proposal strikes a balance between the interests of developed and developing countries, acknowledges historical responsibilities, and offers practical solutions to address climate change while fostering global cooperation and equity.

[2025-04-30 23:16:01] PROPOSAL by EU:
Title: Title: Global Carbon Tax Initiative for Climate Action
Introduction:
Recognizing the urgent need for coordinated international action to combat climate change, the following proposal outlines a Global Carbon Tax Initiative aimed at reducing greenhouse gas emissions and incentivizing the transition to low-carbon economies.

Action Items:
1. Implementation of a progressive global carbon tax framework that takes into account the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities, acknowledging historical contributions to emissions and differing capacities among nations. This framework will ensure that developed nations bear a proportionate burden relative to their historical emissions, while providing flexibility and support for developing countries in their transition to sustainable development.

2. Establishment of a Climate Finance Mechanism to support developing nations in their efforts to implement climate mitigation and adaptation strategies. 3. Formation of an International Oversight Committee comprised of representatives from diverse national perspectives, including developing and developed nations, to monitor the implementation and impact of the Global Carbon Tax Initiative. This committee will oversee the distribution of climate finance, track progress towards emissions reduction targets, and facilitate dialogue and cooperation among nations to address emerging challenges and opportunities in the transition to a low-carbon future.

Political Viability:
This proposal balances the need for ambitious climate action with the recognition of differing national perspectives and capacities. By incorporating flexibility for developing nations and a mechanism for international cooperation and oversight, this Global Carbon Tax Initiative offers a politically viable path towards collective climate action on a global scale.

Thank you.

[2025-04-30 23:16:05] PROPOSAL by India:
Title: Title: Global Carbon Tax Policy Proposal for Equitable Climate Action
Action Items:
1. Implementation of a tiered global carbon tax system based on historical emissions and current capabilities:
   - Developed nations to pay a higher tax rate reflective of their greater historical responsibility for greenhouse gas emissions.
   - Developing nations to have a lower initial tax rate, gradually increasing based on their economic growth and emissions trajectory.

2. Allocation of carbon tax revenue towards a Climate Finance Mechanism:
   - Establish a dedicated fund to support developing nations in their transition to low-carbon economies through technology transfer, capacity-building, and adaptation efforts.
   - Prioritize transparency and accountability in the allocation of funds to ensure effective and equitable utilization.

Perspectives Considered:
- Developed Nations: Acknowledging historical responsibility, developed nations will contribute proportionately higher carbon tax amounts to address climate change.
- Developing Nations: Ensuring flexibility and support, developing nations will benefit from a progressive tax system and dedicated climate finance to promote sustainable development.
- Global Cooperation: Encouraging collaboration among all nations to collectively combat climate change, recognizing the shared responsibility and interconnected nature of environmental challenges.

Political Viability:
This proposal strikes a balance between the interests of developed and developing nations by incorporating the principles of common but differentiated responsibilities. It provides a practical framework for equitable climate action, acknowledging the need for financial support and technology transfer to facilitate a just transition. The tiered tax system and Climate Finance Mechanism offer a pathway towards a sustainable future that respects the unique circumstances of each nation while fostering global cooperation.

We urge all delegates to consider this proposal as a constructive and achievable step in our collective efforts to address climate change effectively and fairly.

Thank you.

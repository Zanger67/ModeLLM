2. Special considerations will be made to avoid disproportionate impacts on these economies.

Political Viability:
This proposal strikes a balance between environmental objectives and economic considerations, addressing the concerns of both developed and developing nations. By incorporating mechanisms for equitable distribution of resources and support for transition, it ensures political viability and broad international cooperation in combating climate change effectively.

Vote: We propose to adopt the Global Carbon Tax Framework for Equitable Climate Action, with the outlined action items to be implemented under the principles of fairness and shared responsibility.

Title: Title: Proposal for a Flexible Global Carbon Tax Framework
Action Items:
1. Implement a tiered global carbon tax system that takes into account the varying levels of economic development and historical emissions of countries. Developed nations would face higher tax rates, while developing nations would have more moderate rates based on their capacity.
2. Establish a Climate Finance Mechanism to support developing nations in transitioning to clean energy technologies and adapting to climate impacts. This fund would be replenished through a portion of the revenue generated from the carbon tax, ensuring equity and solidarity in climate action.
3. Create a review mechanism every five years to assess the effectiveness and fairness of the global carbon tax system, with the opportunity for adjustments based on evolving circumstances and technological advancements.

Considering different national perspectives:
- Developed nations like the EU and the USA would bear a greater burden under this proposal due to their historical emissions and higher economic capacity.
- Developing nations such as China and India would benefit from the flexibility and support provided through the Climate Finance Mechanism, allowing them to pursue sustainable development while contributing to global emission reductions.

Political viability:
This proposal strikes a balance between ambitious climate goals and economic considerations by offering differentiated responsibilities and support for developing nations. By incorporating mechanisms for review and adjustment, it ensures long-term effectiveness and adaptability. The proposal respects the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities, garnering support from a diverse range of countries. Let us work together to adopt this flexible and equitable approach to combat climate change effectively.

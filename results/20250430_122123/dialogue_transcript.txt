[2025-04-30 12:31:06] India:
Honorable Chair, distinguished delegates,

India stands unequivocally committed to addressing the urgent challenge of climate change in a manner that is just, equitable, and inclusive. We recognize the need for robust global action. However, as a nation with one of the world’s largest populations and a significant proportion living in poverty, our priorities must balance environmental stewardship with our right to sustainable development.

We emphasize the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities. Historical emissions from developed nations have significantly contributed to the present crisis, and this must be reflected in any global framework—including proposals for a Global Carbon Tax.

India is steadfast in its climate commitments and has made major strides in expanding renewable energy, enhancing energy efficiency, and advancing adaptation measures. Yet, imposing a uniform global carbon tax risks disproportionately hindering the growth prospects of developing nations, many of whom have historically contributed least to the problem.

Therefore, India calls for:  
1. Recognition of developmental needs and differentiated timelines for developing countries;  
2. Substantial enhancement of climate finance and technology transfer from developed nations, fulfilling both the letter and spirit of prior commitments;  
3. Assurance that any revenues generated from global mechanisms are transparently and equitably allocated for climate adaptation and mitigation in the Global South.  

Let us ensure our actions are guided by fairness and solidarity, enabling all countries to pursue low-carbon pathways without compromising development aspirations.

Thank you.

Title: Title: Equitable Global Carbon Tax Framework and Technological Advancement Proposal
Given the urgent need to tackle climate change, this Committee recognizes the necessity for a global agreement that ensures ecological progress without compromising economic development. With respect to the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities and considering the unique developmental stages of nations, we propose the following: 

1. Establishment of a Global Carbon Tax Framework: This mechanism would entail a flexible, tiered tax structure. 2. Green Technology Fund: Alongside the tax framework, establish a global Green Technology Fund. A proportion of the Global Carbon Tax revenues would be allocated to this fund, focusing on the acquisition, development, and dissemination of clean, energy-efficient technologies. This Fund would prioritize developing nations, ensuring an equitable transition towards sustainable practices.

3. Global Knowledge-Sharing Platform: Develop a global platform that facilitates sharing of information, best practices, and expertise related to climate change mitigation and adaptation. This platform would promote greater global cooperation, fostering a culture of education and inclusivity in our shared fight against climate change. 

By marrying a progressive approach of carbon taxation with the promotion and finance of technological innovation, this proposal strikes a balance between environmental responsibility and economic feasibility. We believe that this proposal can gain broad consensus, providing a foundation for viable, and globally relevant solutions to our climate crisis.

[2025-04-30 12:32:45] PROPOSAL by EU:
Title: **Proposal Title**: *Global Carbon Tax Framework for Equitable Climate Action*
**Proposal Summary**:  
The European Union proposes the adoption of a *Global Carbon Tax Framework* as a key mechanism to accelerate global efforts to mitigate climate change. This framework aims to reflect the principle of *common but differentiated responsibilities* while fostering innovation, fairness, and collective progress.  

**Action Items**:  

1. **Implementation of a Global Carbon Tax with Differentiated Rates**:  
   A global carbon tax will be adopted, with differentiated rates based on countries' historical emissions, current capacities, and levels of development. Developed nations with historically high emissions will contribute at higher rates, while developing nations—especially Least Developed Countries (LDCs)—will begin with lower rates or phased commitments over a longer timeframe.  

2. **Creation of a Global Climate Fund, Financed by Carbon Tax Revenues**:  
   A portion of revenues generated by the tax will be directed into a *Global Climate Fund* administered transparently under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC). This fund will prioritize:  
   - Technology transfer and capacity-building for developing nations, particularly to advance renewable energy and sustainable infrastructure.  
   - Adaptation and resilience funding for countries highly vulnerable to the impacts of climate change.  

3. **Annual Review and Adjustment Mechanism**:  
   An annual review process will be established to assess the tax’s effectiveness, economic impact, and fairness. Adjustments will be made every three years to align rates with emissions reductions, economic development levels, and technological progress.  

**Rationale**:  
This proposal balances ambition and equity by ensuring that high emitters take greater responsibility while offering financial and technological support to developing nations. It fosters collective action and incentivizes sustainable growth, ensuring climate justice for all.  

The European Union urges all parties to support this framework and take concrete steps toward safeguarding our shared future.  

Thank you.

[2025-04-30 12:32:52] PROPOSAL by India:
Title: **Title:**
Equitable Global Carbon Tax Framework for Inclusive Climate Action

**Proposal:**  
Recognizing the urgent need for a just and effective response to climate change, this committee adopts the following framework for a Global Carbon Tax, respecting common but differentiated responsibilities and the diverse economic realities of all nations.

**Action Items:**

1. **Differentiated Implementation Timelines:**  
   The Global Carbon Tax will be applied using a phased approach. Developed countries will adopt the tax within 2 years, reflecting their historic emissions and economic capacity. Developing countries, including emerging economies, may implement the tax on an extended timeline of up to 8 years, with clear milestones reflecting their development needs and capacities.

2. **Climate Finance and Technology Transfer Mechanism:**  
   At least 40% of the revenues generated from the Global Carbon Tax by developed countries shall be placed into an international Climate Solidarity Fund. This fund will be transparently governed and dedicated to supporting adaptation, mitigation, capacity-building, and technology transfer for developing and least-developed countries.

3. **Periodic Review and Flexibility Clause:**  
   A multilateral review mechanism will be established to assess the socio-economic impacts and effectiveness of the tax every three years. This body will have the authority to recommend adjustments to rates, timelines, or exemptions in response to changing national circumstances or unforeseen hardships.

This proposal acknowledges the vital interests of developed nations, recognizes the development aspirations of the Global South, and fosters global collaboration while ensuring no nation is left behind in the transition to a sustainable future.

We call on all delegates to support this balanced, actionable, and politically viable framework.

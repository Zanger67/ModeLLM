=== PROPOSALS ===

[2025-04-30 23:13:57] PROPOSAL by USA:
Title: Title: Global Carbon Tax Initiative for Sustainable Climate Action
Action Items:
1. Implementation of a global carbon tax with differentiated rates based on countries' historical emissions, economic capabilities, and development stages. Developed nations to bear a higher share of the tax burden, reflecting their greater contribution to carbon emissions.
2. Establishment of a Climate Finance Fund to support developing countries in their transition to a low-carbon economy and help them adapt to the impacts of climate change. The fund will provide financial assistance for clean energy projects, technology transfer, and capacity-building initiatives.
3. Formation of an international oversight committee to monitor the implementation and impact of the global carbon tax, ensuring transparency, accountability, and equitable distribution of funds. The committee will include representation from all nations to foster cooperation and compliance.

Considering different national perspectives, the proposal acknowledges the historical responsibility of developed countries in addressing climate change while recognizing the developmental needs and capacities of developing nations. By incorporating differentiated tax rates and a dedicated climate finance mechanism, the proposal aims to strike a balance between environmental objectives and economic considerations, making it politically viable for a diverse range of nations to support.

As the global community comes together to combat climate change, the Global Carbon Tax Initiative offers a pragmatic and equitable approach to reducing emissions, promoting sustainable development, and advancing climate resilience on a global scale. Let us join hands in taking decisive action towards a greener and more prosperous future for all.

[2025-04-30 23:14:02] PROPOSAL by China:
Title: Title: Proposal for a Balanced Global Carbon Tax Framework
Action Items:

1. Implement a Global Carbon Tax that Differentiates Responsibilities: Establish a global carbon tax framework that considers the differentiated responsibilities of developed and developing nations. Developed countries, which have historically contributed more to greenhouse gas emissions, will have higher tax rates compared to developing nations. This approach ensures fairness and equity in addressing climate change.

2. Create a Climate Finance Mechanism: Allocate a portion of the revenue generated from the global carbon tax to a climate finance mechanism that supports developing countries in their efforts to mitigate and adapt to climate change. This mechanism will help bridge the financial gap and promote sustainable development in vulnerable regions.

3. Establish a Review Mechanism: Formulate a periodic review mechanism to assess the effectiveness and impact of the global carbon tax framework. This review will involve all participating nations in evaluating the progress towards emission reduction targets, addressing challenges, and revising tax rates as needed to achieve the desired environmental and economic outcomes.

Considering Different National Perspectives:

- Developed Nations: Recognize the historical responsibility of developed countries in climate change and ensure that their higher tax rates reflect this responsibility while fostering technological innovation and clean energy investments.

- Developing Nations: Provide flexibility to developing nations in complying with the tax framework, acknowledging their need for economic growth and support through climate finance mechanisms.

Politically Viable:

This proposal strikes a balance between the perspectives of developed and developing nations, offering a fair and effective framework to address climate change through a global carbon tax. It aligns with the principles of equity, cooperation, and shared responsibility, making it politically feasible for countries with diverse interests to come together and take meaningful action towards a sustainable future.

Considering Different National Perspectives:
- For developed countries: Prioritize carbon pricing mechanisms as a tool to meet aggressive emission reduction targets, promoting innovation and green investments.
- For developing nations: Provide technical assistance and financial support to facilitate the implementation of carbon pricing, recognizing the need for sustainable development and economic growth.

Political Viability:
This proposal aims to strike a balance between ambitious climate action and economic considerations, addressing the concerns of both developed and developing nations. By promoting a fair and equitable global carbon tax system, this policy offers a practical and politically viable solution to accelerate emissions reductions while fostering sustainable development worldwide.

We call upon all nations to support this Global Climate Action Pact for the implementation of a fair and effective carbon tax regime, signaling a collective commitment to combatting climate change and safeguarding our planet for future generations.

This proposal balances the concerns of developed and developing nations by recognizing historical responsibilities while providing support for sustainable development. It addresses the need for financial assistance through climate finance and acknowledges the varying capacities of countries in combating climate change.

[2025-04-30 11:55:15] China:
Thank you, Honorable Chair, distinguished delegates,

The People’s Republic of China approaches today’s discussion with a spirit of constructive cooperation and pragmatism. We acknowledge the grave threat that climate change poses to all humanity, and we remain committed to ambitious action consistent with the Paris Agreement and our own national objectives.

China recognizes the merit in exploring global mechanisms, such as a carbon tax, to reduce greenhouse gas emissions. However, we must emphasize the principle of Common But Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities (CBDR–RC). It is essential that any global policy recognizes the fundamental difference between developed and developing nations in terms of historical emissions and stage of economic development.

China believes that developing countries, which are still addressing poverty alleviation and economic growth, must be granted greater flexibility. We urge any framework to:  
1. Allow developing nations additional time and support to adapt to new measures,  
2. Provide for financial and technological assistance from developed nations, and  
3. Ensure climate policy does not hinder the right to development and poverty eradication.

We are ready to actively participate in dialogue and work toward ambitious global climate goals. But we must ensure fairness—so that climate solutions foster, rather than impede, inclusive economic and social advancement.

Thank you.

Implement a climate finance mechanism from revenue generated by the carbon tax and developed nations' contributions to assist developing nations in their transition to a sustainable economy. Support will include technical assistance, capacity building, and technology transfer to foster growth, reduce poverty, and adapt to the impacts of climate change.

This proposal aims to balance environmental needs with the economic realities of nations, addressing the concerns about strict regulations while recognizing the gravity of climate change. It emphasizes the American belief in the intersection of innovation, market solutions, and global cooperation, to positively shape our shared climate future.

Title: **Proposal Title:**
Equitable Global Carbon Tax Framework for Sustainable Development

**Preamble:**  
Recognizing the shared challenge of climate change, reaffirming the Paris Agreement principles—especially Common But Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities (CBDR-RC), and acknowledging varying levels of development and historical emissions, this committee proposes the following measures for an effective and fair global carbon tax.

**Action Items:**

1. **Phased and Differentiated Implementation:**

2. **Climate Finance and Technology Transfer Mechanism:**  
   Develop a dedicated fund, supported by revenues from the carbon tax levied on developed nations, to directly finance mitigation, adaptation, and clean technology transfer in developing and least-developed countries. 3. **Regular Review and Adjustment:**  
   Institute a mechanism for five-year reviews—aligned with global stocktake procedures—allowing adjustment of tax rates, implementation timelines, and support measures, engaging all Parties to respond flexibly to changing economic, scientific, and technological circumstances.

**Conclusion:**  
This framework balances urgent climate action with equity and the right to development. It ensures ambitious commitments from developed countries, delivers supporting resources to developing nations, and provides flexibility and transparency, making a global carbon tax both effective and politically sustainable for all Parties.

**We urge all delegations to support this proposal in the spirit of shared but differentiated responsibility.**

Title: Title: “Global Carbon Tax Framework: An Equitable and Ambitious Path Forward”
Proposal Description (Under 300 words)

1. Phased, Gradual Implementation  
All nations shall adopt a globally harmonized carbon tax starting at a modest rate, with annual increments. [2025-04-30 11:57:17] PROPOSAL by India:
Title: **Proposal Title:**
*Equitable Climate Action Through a Phased and Supportive Global Carbon Pricing Framework*

**Preamble:**  
Acknowledging the urgent need for global action on climate change, this proposal aims to establish a fair and effective approach to carbon pricing that recognizes the principle of *Common But Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities (CBDR-RC)*. It balances climate ambitions with the developmental needs of nations, ensuring fairness, inclusion, and support for sustainable transitions.

**Action Items:**  
1. **Phased Implementation of a Global Carbon Pricing Framework:**  
   - Developed nations shall implement mandatory carbon pricing mechanisms (e.g., carbon taxes or cap-and-trade systems) immediately, with an initial minimum rate to be agreed upon by consensus.  
   - Developing nations will adopt voluntary carbon pricing mechanisms for a transitionary period of 10 years, during which they will set their own rates, tailored to their economic and developmental circumstances. Compliance will be supported by global collaboration, focusing on reducing inequities in climate impacts.  

2. **Commitment to Climate Finance and Technology Transfer:**  
   - Developed nations shall commit $200 billion annually by 2030 toward climate finance for developing countries, to fund clean energy transitions, adaptation efforts, and capacity building.  
   - A global platform for technology transfer will be established, providing affordable access to green technologies for all nations. Developed nations will contribute resources and expertise, waiving intellectual property barriers for critical climate solutions when necessary.

3. **Protection of Vulnerable Populations and Just Transitions:**  
   - A global framework will ensure that vulnerable populations in all nations, including workers in carbon-intensive industries, are protected through social safety nets, education, and reskilling programs funded through climate finance mechanisms.

**Conclusion:**  
This equitable framework provides an actionable pathway to address the climate crisis while acknowledging the diverse realities of nations. It seeks to harmonize global efforts toward a sustainable future without compromising the rights and aspirations of developing populations.  

Thank you.

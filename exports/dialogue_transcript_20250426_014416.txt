=== PROPOSALS ===

[2025-04-26 01:39:29] PROPOSAL by USA:
Title: **Proposal Title:**
Global Carbon Tax Accord: Coordinated Action Against Climate Change

**Purpose:**  
To establish a fair, effective, and politically viable global carbon tax framework, incentivizing emissions reduction while respecting different countries' economic circumstances.

**Proposed Action Items:**  

1. **Tiered Global Carbon Tax:**  
  - Implement a universal carbon tax with differentiated rates based on countries’ income levels and historical emissions.  
  - High-income and high-emission countries adopt an initial tax rate of $50/ton CO₂e, increasing $10/year; middle-income countries start at $20/ton CO₂e, increasing $5/year; low-income countries may delay implementation up to 5 years, with technical and financial support.

2. **Revenue Redistribution & Support:**  
  - At least 25% of carbon tax revenues from high- and middle-income countries are pooled in a Global Climate Fund.  
  - This fund finances clean technology transfer, adaptation measures, and sustainable development in low-income and climate-vulnerable nations.

3. **Border Carbon Adjustments:**  
  - To ensure fair competition, implement carbon border adjustment mechanisms. Countries not participating or with weaker standards pay a tariff on carbon-intensive imports, incentivizing broader adoption.

**Consideration of Perspectives:**  
- Developed countries acknowledge responsibility with higher rates and funding commitments.  
- Developing nations retain flexibility and gain support to avoid growth constraints.  
- All parties benefit from predictable rules, technological progress, and revenue recycling.

**Political Viability:**  
- The tiered, flexible approach recognizes global disparities, learning from existing models (e.g., EU ETS, Canada’s system).  
- Shared revenue ensures fairness and global buy-in, while border adjustments drive universal participation.
- The system is structured for incremental implementation and broad coalition-building.

**Recommendation:**  
Adopt this Global Carbon Tax Accord to align climate ambition with economic realities—delivering real emissions cuts, climate justice, and sustainable growth.

[2025-04-26 01:39:36] PROPOSAL by China:
Title: **Proposal: Establishing a Globally Coordinated Carbon Tax to Combat Climate Change**
**Action Items:**  
1. **Adopt a Tiered Global Carbon Tax Framework**: Introduce a universal carbon tax starting at $25 per metric ton of CO₂ emissions, scaling up annually by $10 until 2050. The tax structure will account for varying national economic capacities by offering lower initial rates for developing nations, with technical and financial aid to facilitate adaptation.  

2. **Create a Climate Solidarity Fund**: Allocate 25% of revenues from the carbon tax into an international fund dedicated to financing renewable energy infrastructure, climate adaptation projects, and technology transfer in low-income countries. This ensures equity while addressing the disproportionate burdens of climate change.  

3. **Implement Border Carbon Adjustments (BCAs)**: To maintain competitiveness and encourage global compliance, levy tariffs on imported goods from countries that do not participate in the carbon tax system. Exemptions will be provided to least-developed countries to ensure fairness and promote engagement.  

**National Perspectives**:  
This proposal balances economic development with climate responsibility. Developed nations, as top emitters, bear a higher initial burden, while developing nations are given financial support and lower tax thresholds to grow sustainably. BCAs ensure a level playing field, reducing resistance from major economies by avoiding carbon leakage.  

**Political Viability**:  
The tiered structure and solidarity fund address equity concerns, increasing buy-in from developing countries. BCAs help align economic incentives for non-participating nations. By emphasizing both fairness and global economic stability, this proposal appeals to a broad coalition of governments, businesses, and civil society stakeholders, making it attainable in multilateral frameworks like the United Nations or G20.  

This globally coordinated carbon tax is a pragmatic, adaptive, and just approach to reducing emissions and mitigating climate change impacts.

[2025-04-26 01:39:45] PROPOSAL by EU:
Title: GLOBAL CARBON TAX ACCORD
Proposal:  
This proposal sets forth a globally coordinated carbon tax aimed at reducing greenhouse gas emissions, financing clean energy transitions, and promoting equitable development. It balances the economic realities of both developed and developing nations, ensuring a fair pathway to significant emissions reductions.

Action Items:  
1. Establish a Uniform Minimum Carbon Tax  
   All signatory countries agree to a minimum levy of US$30 per metric ton of CO₂-equivalent emissions, with the rate adjusted every five years based on scientific and economic assessments. Nations retain the option to impose higher taxes if feasible.

2. Create a Global Climate Equity Fund  
   A portion (at least 25%) of collected carbon tax revenues will flow into a dedicated international fund. This fund will support clean energy projects, adaptation efforts in vulnerable nations, and workforce transition programs in coal-reliant regions.

3. Implement Gradual Phase-In  
   To ensure political viability, the tax would start at US$15 per metric ton in developing countries and reach the uniform rate within a decade, allowing time for economic adjustment and technology transfer.

Rationale:  
By jointly committing to a minimum tax and reinvesting revenues in climate solutions, nations can reduce emissions while addressing economic disparities. This consensus-based approach encourages broad participation, ensuring the policy is both effective and equitable.

This proposal is hereby submitted for consideration and ratification.

[2025-04-26 01:39:56] PROPOSAL by India:
Title: Title: Proposal for Implementation of a Global Carbon Tax Policy
1. Action Item 1: Establish a Global Carbon Pricing Mechanism - Develop a global scale standardized carbon pricing system under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC). This mechanism will impose a significant financial cost on carbon emitter countries proportionate to their carbon emissions. The revenue collected from this mechanism will be directed to fund renewable energy projects and climate change mitigations across the globe.

2. Action Item 2: Equalization Measures - Implement an 'Equalization Fund' to assist developing countries struggling to manage their emission costs. This fund, under the scrutiny of UNFCCC, will ensure that the proposed carbon tax does not hamper the socio-economic progression of these countries. The primary sources of this fund would be contributions from developed nations and a portion of the revenue generated from the carbon tax.

3. Action Item 3: Incentives and Sanctions - To motivate the adherence of countries to the proposed carbon tax, a reward/sanction system should be in place. Nations significantly decreasing their carbon emissions will receive benefits in the form of technology transfer, funding, or assistance in renewable energy projects. On the contrary, countries failing to meet their carbon reduction commitments may face sanctions.

Nation-wise perspectives will be given due consideration while implementing these actions, ensuring that the principles of equity and climate justice are not compromised. Strategically, this proposal aims to create a core financial disincentive for carbon pollution, thus steering global economies towards climate-resilient pathways. Despite the challenges anticipated, we believe the execution of these action items will provide a bold, constructive response to climate changes and position us on a healthier, safer planet.

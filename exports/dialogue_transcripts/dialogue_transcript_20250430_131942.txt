[2025-04-30 13:13:45] PROPOSAL by USA:
Title: **Title:**
Flexible Framework for Global Carbon Pricing and Clean Technology Cooperation

**Proposal:**  
Recognizing the urgent need for international climate action and the diverse economic realities of United Nations member states, this committee proposes the following:

1. **Adopt a Flexible Global Carbon Pricing Framework:**  
Participating nations will implement a nationally determined carbon pricing mechanism—such as a carbon tax, emissions trading system, or equivalent policy—with a minimum effective price to be reviewed and adjusted every five years by an international panel. Countries retain the flexibility to choose and design their approach to best fit their economic structures and development needs.

2. **Establish a Global Clean Technology Fund:**  
A dedicated fund will be created, supported by revenues from national carbon pricing mechanisms and contributions from developed countries. This fund will prioritize technology transfer, capacity building, and support for climate mitigation and adaptation projects in developing nations.

3. **Ensure Equity, Transparency, and Review:**  
All participating countries will commit to transparent reporting of carbon pricing revenues and emissions reductions, with biennial peer review. Special consideration will be given to least developed countries and economies in transition, allowing for differentiated responsibilities and extended timelines, ensuring no country is left behind.

This proposal respects national sovereignty, supports ambitious climate goals through proven market-based solutions, and fosters meaningful North-South cooperation. It seeks to align the interests of both advanced and emerging economies for the benefit of our planet and future generations.

**We invite member states to support this balanced, pragmatic, and equitable pathway towards significant global emission reductions.**

[2025-04-30 13:14:06] PROPOSAL by EU:
Title: **Proposal Title:** *Establishment of a Fair and Equitable Global Carbon Tax Framework*
**Preamble:**  
Recognizing the urgent need for bold collective action to address the climate crisis and reaffirming the shared commitments under the Paris Agreement, this proposal seeks to establish a globally coordinated carbon tax system that is ambitious, equitable, and adaptable to diverse national circumstances.

**Action Items:**  
1. **Adoption of a Tiered Global Carbon Tax System**:  
   All nations shall implement a base carbon tax starting at $50 per metric ton of CO2 emissions, with adjustments based on *Common but Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities (CBDR-RC)* principles:  
   - Developed countries will adopt higher rates (e.g., $75 per ton) recognizing historical emissions.  
   - Developing nations will begin at the base rate or with phased adoption, allowing economic transitions. Least Developed Countries (LDCs) and Small Island Developing States (SIDS) will be exempt from mandatory taxes and supported with additional climate finance.  

2. **Creation of a Global Climate Fund for Justice and Transition**:  
   A minimum of 50% of revenues generated from the global carbon tax will be pooled into a Global Climate Fund administered transparently by an international body. Funds will:  
   - Support renewable energy transition and infrastructure in developing countries.  
   - Assist vulnerable communities in adapting to climate change impacts.  
   - Facilitate technology transfer and capacity-building initiatives.  

3. **National Sovereignty and Flexibility**:  
   Nations will define the precise mechanisms for implementing the carbon tax within their jurisdictions, provided they meet agreed minimum targets. Countries may choose to integrate the tax into existing systems (e.g., emissions trading schemes) to ensure economic compatibility and market stability.

**Conclusion:**  
This framework balances the urgent need for significant emissions reductions with respect for national circumstances. It fosters trust through equitable measures, delivers crucial climate finance, and lays the foundation for shared prosperity in a sustainable future.  

This proposal is now open for consideration and approval.

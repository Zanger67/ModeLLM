Title: **Title:**
Global Climate Solidarity Pact: Equitable Implementation of a Binding International Carbon Tax

**Preamble:**  
Acknowledging the urgent threat of climate change and the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities, this committee strides toward a fair, effective, and economically sound solution to reduce global greenhouse gas emissions.

**Action Items:**

1. **Adopt a Binding International Carbon Tax Framework:**  
All participating countries shall commit to implementing a minimum carbon tax, with a globally agreed baseline rate (e.g., USD $30/ton CO₂ equivalent) by 2028. Developed countries shall implement at the baseline rate or higher by 2026, while developing countries may phase in the tax by 2030, reflecting national circumstances.

2. **Establish an Equitable Climate Solidarity Fund:**  
A significant portion of revenues collected from the carbon tax by developed nations will be allocated to an international Climate Solidarity Fund. This fund will finance climate mitigation, adaptation projects, and technology transfer in developing countries, ensuring they are not economically disadvantaged and can pursue sustainable development.

3. **Periodic Review and Flexibility Mechanism:**  
A Science-Policy Panel under UNFCCC will reassess tax levels, implementation timelines, and fund allocations every five years, enabling adjustments to reflect economic realities, technological progress, and national circumstances. Countries facing extraordinary developmental or economic challenges may apply for temporary exemptions or support measures through an independent review process.

**Conclusion:**  
This proposal ensures an effective, fair, and binding climate action mechanism, balancing environmental ambition with economic equity. It represents a compromise reflecting diverse national realities while upholding the planetary imperative to act. The European Union urges all delegates to unite behind this pragmatic and forward-looking pact.

Title: **Proposal Title:**
**Equitable Global Framework for a Carbon Tax and Climate Finance**  

**Preamble:**  
Recognizing the urgent need for collective climate action and the principle of Common but Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities (CBDR-RC), this proposal aims to address global greenhouse gas emissions with equity at its core. It acknowledges the historical responsibility of developed nations, the development aspirations of the Global South, and the importance of providing financial and technical support to ensure a just transition for all.

**Action Items:**  

1. **Design an Equitable Global Carbon Tax Framework:**  
   A global carbon tax will be implemented with differentiated rates reflecting each nation’s historical emissions and current development status.  
   - *Developed nations* will bear higher tax rates due to their historical responsibility and stronger economic capacity.  
   - *Developing nations*, including Least Developed Countries (LDCs), will either pay lower rates or be granted exemptions to ensure their development priorities are not disrupted.  
   - Funds generated from this tax will be channeled into a dedicated Global Climate Justice Fund.  

2. **Establish a Global Climate Justice Fund (GCJF):**  
   Administered by the United Nations or a similar multilateral body, the GCJF will support:  
   - Climate adaptation and mitigation projects in developing nations.  
   - Technology transfer to promote renewable energy and sustainable practices.  
   - Capacity-building programs to empower nations with low institutional infrastructure for climate actions.  

3. **Mandatory Climate Finance Contributions from Developed Nations:**  
   Developed nations will be required to commit 1-2% of GDP annually to climate finance. This funding will be independent of payments into the GCJF to ensure predictable and adequate financial flows for climate action in the Global South.

**Conclusion:**  
This proposal ensures global responsibility-sharing while respecting the development needs of the Global South. It strengthens international solidarity and creates a fair pathway to achieve ambitious climate goals.

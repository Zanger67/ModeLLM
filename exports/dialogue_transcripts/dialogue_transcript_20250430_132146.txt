[2025-04-30 13:15:57] PROPOSAL by USA:
Title: **Title: A Framework for a Fair and Flexible Global Carbon Pricing Mechanism**
**Preamble:**  
Acknowledging the urgent need to combat climate change, the global community recognizes the potential of carbon pricing to incentivize emissions reductions. This proposal outlines a fair, flexible, and scalable framework to guide nations toward sustainable economic growth while addressing climate objectives.  

**Action Items:**  

1. **Adopt a Tiered Carbon Pricing Mechanism, Reflecting National Circumstances:**  
   Implement a global carbon pricing framework allowing nations to adopt policies—such as carbon taxes or cap-and-trade systems—that match their economic and developmental realities. For developed economies with higher historic emissions, advanced carbon pricing of $50–$100/ton CO2 must be phased in by 2030. Developing and emerging economies start at modest pricing ($10–$30/ton CO2) and gradually increase, with technical and financial support.

2. **Climate Finance and Technology Transfer for Developing Nations:**  
   Establish a dedicated global fund—financed by contributions from developed nations and revenues from carbon pricing mechanisms—to support low-income and developing economies. Funds will boost renewable energy projects, climate adaptation initiatives, and access to affordable clean technologies. This ensures global equity and addresses disparities in capacity.

3. **Transparent Monitoring and Verification System:**  
   Create an internationally governed system to track carbon pricing implementation and emissions reduction progress. Nations will report annually, with flexibility for national contexts, but uniform transparency standards. Non-compliance may result in tailored diplomatic or financial consequences.

**Rationale:**  
This proposal bridges the gap between ambitious climate action (EU-led) and developmental equity concerns (India and China). By recognizing economic diversity, emphasizing innovation, and respecting national sovereignty, it secures practicality, broad support, and attention to justice.

**Conclusion:**  
This framework allows nations to meet shared climate goals while fostering collaboration, growth, and innovation. We urge all delegates to support this inclusive, achievable, and impactful approach.  

Thank you.

[2025-04-30 13:16:27] PROPOSAL by EU:
Title: GLOBAL CARBON TAX PROTOCOL FOR EQUITABLE CLIMATE ACTION
Action Item 1: Establish a Tiered Carbon Tax  
A legally binding global carbon tax framework will be implemented, with tiered rates reflecting each nation’s historical emissions and current development stage. Developed nations, with higher past contributions to climate change, will pay a higher tax rate. Emerging economies will pay a moderate tier, receiving technological and financial support to facilitate a just transition.

Action Item 2: Create a Green Transition Fund  
Revenue from the carbon tax will be allocated to a dedicated Green Transition Fund, managed jointly by an international body. This fund will finance technology transfer, renewable energy projects, and capacity-building in developing nations, ensuring they are not disproportionately burdened and can meet growth priorities.

Action Item 3: Mandate Transparent Review and Accountability  
All participating nations will submit annual progress reports on emissions reductions and tax revenues. A global review panel, under the auspices of the United Nations, will verify data, recommend adjustments to tax tiers if necessary, and address non-compliance through graduated penalties or incentives.

By providing differentiated responsibilities, robust financial support, and enforceable transparency, this Global Carbon Tax Protocol balances urgency with equity. It delivers a politically viable solution that spurs environmental innovation, supports emerging economies’ development needs, and ensures shared responsibility across all participant nations.

Title: **Title:**
Equitable Implementation Framework for a Global Carbon Tax

**Proposal:**  
Recognizing the urgent need for coordinated global action on climate change, and acknowledging the diversity of national circumstances and developmental needs, we propose the following framework for the adoption of a Global Carbon Tax:

**1. Differentiated Carbon Tax Structure:**  
Participating nations will adopt a carbon tax consistent with their stage of development, historical emissions, and current capabilities. Developed countries shall implement higher, immediate carbon pricing, while developing countries may apply a lower rate with a gradual escalation linked to measurable financial and technological support received.

**2. Dedicated Climate Finance & Technology Transfer Facility:**  
Establish a new, legally binding international fund—separate from and in addition to existing mechanisms—to channel targeted climate finance and advanced technologies from developed to developing nations. **3. Transparent Review and Adjustment Mechanism:**  
Create a biennial review process under the auspices of the UNFCCC, ensuring transparency and flexibility. This process will assess the effectiveness, equity, and support provided, allowing for periodic recalibration of national carbon tax rates and international support levels based on progress and changing national circumstances.

This proposal upholds environmental ambition while respecting equity and national sovereignty, ensuring that climate action and development progress go hand in hand. We urge all parties to support this balanced and practical path forward.

India acknowledges the strong ambition and structured approach of your Global Carbon Tax Protocol, notably its tiered carbon tax and the establishment of a legally binding framework. We particularly commend the proposed Green Transition Fund, which aligns with our own call for a dedicated facility enabling technology transfer and climate finance to developing nations. This shared emphasis on robust support for emerging economies is a constructive foundation for collaboration.

However, India remains cautious regarding the mandatory nature of binding commitments, especially where developing countries’ growth trajectories could be constrained without ironclad guarantees of timely financial and technological support. We contend that meaningful climate ambition must be balanced with the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities, holding developed nations to a higher initial standard in both taxation and support contributions.

As a constructive path forward, India proposes jointly designing the governance structure of the Green Transition Fund to ensure equitable access and accountability, integrating oversight mechanisms from both our proposals. We are open to exploring an annual transparency process paired with biennial recalibrations, merging your desire for frequent reporting with our need for predictable adjustment and flexibility.

By marrying enforceable finance with differentiated commitments, and prioritizing transparent but adaptable oversight, we believe India and the EU can champion a truly fair and effective global carbon mechanism.

We look forward to deepening this collaboration in the spirit of equity and shared responsibility.

Thank you.
